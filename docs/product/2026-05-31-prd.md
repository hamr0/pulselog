# pulselog PRD — requirements and locked decisions

Source: `docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md`. Citations are `(file:start-end)`.

## 1. Problem

- addypin, plato and gitdone each grew two kinds of hand-written ops code, re-implemented per app and drifting (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:42-43).
- "Is it up right now?" is a cron/timer script that curls `/health` and checks systemd units, disk, TLS expiry and mail queue. It is silent when green, emails on failure, and was written about 3 times (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:45-47).
- "How is it trending?" is a stats subsystem: a daily collector snapshots numbers to JSONL and a weekly digest emails a week-over-week table (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:48-50).
- The digest renderer is duplicated nearly verbatim between `plato/bin/stats-weekly.js` and `gitdone/app/bin/stats-weekly.js`. The collector is rewritten per app (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:50-53).
- Both jobs observe a running app from outside, on a schedule. flightlog records errors from inside, and nothing generalizes the outside view (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:55-58).

## 2. Goal

One zero-dependency, scheduled, external watcher, the outside sibling to flightlog. It has three modes, one config and one JSONL dialect (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:62-63).

- **health**: generalizes the probe-and-alert script, already built (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:65).
- **digest**: the operator declares the metrics list. pulselog owns collect, snapshot, history, week-over-week and email. The metrics list is the only per-app customization (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:66-69).
- **backup**: the operator declares a dump command and a rolling window. pulselog owns stage, `tar`, atomic write, retention, integrity floor, JSONL and alert. It was designed to target `0.2.0` (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:70-74).
- Apps port to pulselog after it is complete, and pulselog never edits their repos (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:76).

## 3. Success criteria

1. addypin's and plato's health scripts express fully as a `checks` config, with no bespoke bash left (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:80-81).
2. A new app gets weekly stats by declaring a metrics list, with no `stats.js` or `stats-weekly.js`. It reproduces the existing plato/gitdone digest output (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:82-84).
3. The weekly email says which kind of error occurred (api vs mail-auth) and flags any that crossed the threshold, without emailing a log line (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:85-87).
4. Zero production dependencies (`node:*` plus global `fetch`), Node 18 or later (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:88).
5. Health is silent on green. Digest always sends, with an opt-in skip when flat (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:89).
6. Only aggregate integers and error names leave the box. Message bodies and stacks are never emailed (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:90-91).
7. It ships JSDoc-generated types, a `pulselog.context.md` adopter contract, CI and a manual OIDC publish, the same bar as flightlog (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:92-93).

## 4. What pulselog is NOT

The refusals are the product (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:95).

- **Not a daemon or scheduler.** You bring cron or a systemd timer. It runs once and exits, and WoW history lives in the JSONL (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:97-98).
- **Not a log aggregator, shipper or SIEM.** The digest reports numbers and error names, never log lines (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:99-101).
- **Not a metrics database, dashboard or TSDB.** It appends JSONL and renders a plain-text table, with no UI, server, query layer or charts (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:102-103).
- **Not an uptime SaaS.** It is local, with no account, telemetry or phone-home (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:104).
- **Not an alerting platform.** One email per run, with no routing, escalation, paging, on-call, dedup or rate policy (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:105-106).
- **Not a transport.** Shipping the JSONL off-box is a separate, consent-gated layer you build. flightlog's `examples/ship.js` is the reference (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:107-108).
- **Not a PII collector.** It stores only the integer a metric command returns. "Unique customers" means the adopter's query does `count(distinct ...)` (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:109-112).
- **Never emails raw logs.** flightlog is private by default. The optional enrichment is an aggregate: the 7-day error count, top error names with counts, and a per-name threshold flag. Messages and stacks are never emailed (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:113-119).
- **Not flightlog.** flightlog captures from inside and pulselog observes from outside. They are separate tools and files sharing one dialect (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:120-121).
- **No HTTP body assertion.** An `http` check verifies status code only. plato's `db_writable` goes through the `command` escape hatch (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:122-124).
- **Not a backup engine.** Backup wraps the adopter's dump command and guarantees only that the dump exited 0 and produced an archive of at least `minBytes`, not that it restores. Restore-testing, off-host copy and encryption stay the operator's (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:125-130).

## 5. Decisions (locked)

Each row is a decision and its rationale from the table (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:134-153).

| # | Decision | Cite |
|---|----------|------|
| D1 | One tool, three modes: `health` (default), `digest` (`--digest`), `backup` (`--backup`). All reuse `sink.js` and `email.js`. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:136) |
| D2 | Modes stay crisp, with separate config sections and invocation, so the chatty digest never erodes silent-on-green. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:137) |
| D3 | pulselog owns collection too: collect, snapshot, history, WoW, email. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:138) |
| D4 | One weekly run is collect plus digest, with one line per week and no DB or lockfile. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:139) |
| D5 | A metric source is a `command` that prints one integer. Integers only. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:140) |
| D6 | Digest always sends, with opt-in `skipIfFlat` (every delta 0 and no flightlog flag). | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:141) |
| D7 | flightlog enrichment is optional: count, top error names and a threshold flag, weekly over 7 days. It is persisted in the history line and never contains messages or stacks. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:142) |
| D8 | Alert transport is system `mail`/`sendmail`, with `msmtp` suggested as an addon. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:143) |
| D9 | One config, with `checks` and `digest` in a single `pulselog.config.json`. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:144) |
| D10 | Exit 0 on a health or stat failure. Exit 1 only on a run or config error. **Amended in 0.8.0 (user-approved):** health and digest also exit 1 when a send was attempted and every delivery path failed; see wiki/alert-delivery-decisions.md. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:145, 552-557) |
| D11 | Backups are a third mode, not a sibling lib. The envelope was hand-rolled at least 3 times. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:146) |
| D12 | The envelope owns the lifecycle and stages `db`, `include` and `command` sources into `$PULSELOG_STAGE`. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:147) |
| D13 | Rolling retention by `keepLast` and/or `keepDays`. It deletes only its own `<name>-<stamp>.tar.gz` files and never rotates after a failed or empty archive. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:148) |
| D14 | Integrity floor: atomic `.tmp` to `mv`, and the archive must be at least `minBytes`. It asserts the dump ran, not restorability. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:149) |
| D15 | A failed backup exits 1 (ratified 2026-05-31), unlike a failed health check, because a silently missing backup must never be quiet. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:150) |
| D16 | Built-in safe-default dumps (ratified 2026-05-31): `sqlite` via node:sqlite `VACUUM INTO`, `postgres` via `pg_dump -Fc`, `mysql`/MariaDB via `mysqldump --single-transaction`. `command` is the opt-out. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:151) |
| D17 | `include` copies static files into the stage. Entries are required by default or `{path,optional:true}`, and symlinks are preserved. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:152) |
| D18 | The backup payload is treated as secret-bearing: archive `0600`, `dir`/stage `0700`, DB passwords via env and never argv, collisions fail loud, single-line email headers. It came from the security audit and was fixed before `0.2.0`. | (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:153) |

Key rationales:

- Health observes, so finding a service down is success (exit 0). Backup produces, so failing to produce is a run failure (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:150).
- "`command` exited 0" alone is false confidence, since a truncated `pg_dump` exits 0 under disk-full. The size floor and recorded `bytes` make a broken backup visible (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:149).
- D16 curates safe defaults for common OSS engines, not a general engine matrix. Exotic stores stay `command` (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:147, docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:151).
- Before the D18 fixes, the archive was `0644` and a postgres password sat in `pg_dump` argv, readable via `ps`. Each fix was validated with a repro and verified against real Postgres and MariaDB (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:153).

## 10. Naming

- `healthwatch` was a rushed, generic placeholder. It was renamed to `pulselog` (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:370-372).
- The name is coined, free on npm (reserved at `0.0.1`), and pairs with flightlog in the same `-log` family and JSONL dialect. The tree and local dir were renamed 2026-05-31 (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:372-374).

## 14. Provenance (process)

- The PRD was spun off from the flightlog session on 2026-05-31, and flightlog is the proven template (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:469).
- pulselog inherits the doctrine: mechanism in the lib, policy and data in the adopter; the JSONL is the interface; refusals are the product; lean wrapper, never complicate (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:469-472).
- Decisions D1-D10 and the section 4 refusals were litigated with the user before the PRD (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:472-473).
- The privacy and no-email-of-logs refusals are load-bearing. They are why the digest is safe to keep and mail under each product's privacy claims (docs/archive/2026-05-31-prd.md:473-475).
